## Definition
Gastritis is inflammation of the stomach lining. A peptic ulcer is a sore in the lining of the stomach or the first part of the small intestine.

## Causes
Helicobacter pylori infection, frequent use of anti-inflammatory pain medicines (such as ibuprofen or aspirin), alcohol and smoking.

## Symptoms
Burning or gnawing pain in the upper belly, bloating, nausea and feeling full quickly.

## How it is investigated
Tests for H. pylori (breath, stool or blood) and sometimes endoscopy.

## Treatment approaches
Medicines that reduce stomach acid and, if H. pylori is found, a course of antibiotics, prescribed by a health worker.

## When to seek medical attention
Emergency for vomiting blood, black tarry stools, or sudden severe abdominal pain.

## Definition
Appendicitis is inflammation of the appendix, a small pouch attached to the large intestine.

## Symptoms
Pain that often starts around the navel and moves to the lower right belly, getting worse over hours, with loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting and fever.

## How it is investigated
Examination, blood tests and ultrasound or CT scan.

## Treatment approaches
Usually surgery to remove the appendix (appendectomy); some cases are treated with antibiotics.

## When to seek medical attention
Seek emergency care: a burst appendix is dangerous.

## Definition
A UTI is an infection anywhere in the urinary system, most often the bladder. It is more common in women.

## Symptoms
Burning when passing urine, needing to pass urine often, cloudy or smelly urine, and lower belly pain. Kidney infection adds fever, back or side pain and vomiting.

## How it is investigated
Urine test (urinalysis and sometimes culture).

## Treatment approaches
Antibiotics prescribed by a health worker when needed.

## Prevention
Drink enough water, do not hold urine for long, and wipe front to back.

## When to seek medical attention
See a health worker for UTI symptoms, and urgently for fever, back pain, vomiting, or symptoms in pregnancy.

## Definition
Chronic kidney disease means the kidneys are damaged and slowly lose their ability to filter blood over months or years.

## Causes and risk factors
Diabetes and high blood pressure are the most common causes. Others: kidney infections, inherited conditions and some medicines.

## Symptoms
Often none until late. Later: swelling of the legs, tiredness, poor appetite, itching, and passing less urine.

## How it is investigated
Blood tests (creatinine, eGFR) and urine tests for protein.

## Treatment approaches
Controlling blood pressure and blood sugar, medicines, diet advice, and dialysis or transplant for kidney failure.

## When to seek medical attention
Seek care for swelling, much less urine, or if you have diabetes or high blood pressure and have not had kidney tests.

Kidney stones are hard deposits that form in the kidneys from minerals in urine.

## Symptoms
Sudden severe pain in the side or back that may spread to the groin, blood in urine, nausea and vomiting.

## Treatment approaches
Pain relief and fluids; larger stones may need procedures in hospital.

## When to seek medical attention
Seek urgent care for severe pain, fever with pain, or inability to pass urine.

## Definition
Anaemia means there are not enough healthy red blood cells or haemoglobin to carry oxygen around the body.

## Causes
Iron deficiency (most common), malaria, worm infections, blood loss (including heavy periods), pregnancy, poor diet, and inherited conditions such as sickle cell disease.

## Symptoms
Tiredness, weakness, pale skin and inner eyelids, shortness of breath and dizziness.

## How it is investigated
A blood test for haemoglobin (full blood count) and tests to find the cause.

## Treatment approaches
Treating the cause, iron and other supplements as advised by a health worker, and blood transfusion in severe cases.

## Prevention
Iron-rich foods, iron and folic acid supplements in pregnancy as recommended, malaria prevention and deworming.

## When to seek medical attention
Seek urgent care for severe breathlessness, chest pain or fainting.

## Definition
Sickle cell disease is an inherited blood disorder in which red blood cells become curved and stiff, block small blood vessels and break down early.

## Symptoms
Anaemia, episodes of severe pain (crises), swelling of hands and feet in young children, frequent infections and delayed growth.

## Treatment approaches
Lifelong care at specialist clinics, infection prevention, pain management, fluids, and medicines such as hydroxyurea for some patients.

## When to seek medical attention
Emergency for fever, severe pain, chest pain or difficulty breathing, sudden weakness, or a rapidly enlarging spleen in a child.

## Triggers
Soap, heat, sweat, rough fabrics, stress and allergies.

## Definition
Fungal skin infections such as ringworm (tinea) cause itchy, ring-shaped, scaly patches. They are not caused by worms.

## Spread
Through skin contact with infected people or animals and shared towels, combs or clothes.

## Treatment approaches
Antifungal creams or, for scalp or nail infections, tablets prescribed by a health worker. Keep skin clean and dry.

## When to seek medical attention
See a health worker if the rash spreads, is on the scalp, or does not improve.

## Definition
Depression is a common mental health condition with a low mood or loss of interest lasting most of the day, nearly every day, for at least two weeks.

## Symptoms
Sadness, loss of interest, tiredness, poor sleep or sleeping too much, changes in appetite, poor concentration, feelings of guilt or worthlessness, and thoughts of death or suicide.

## Treatment approaches
Talking therapies, support, and sometimes antidepressant medicines prescribed by a health worker. Depression is treatable.

## When to seek medical attention
Talk to a health worker if symptoms last more than two weeks. If someone has thoughts of suicide or self-harm, get help immediately from emergency services or a health facility, and do not leave them alone.

## Definition
Anxiety disorders involve excessive fear and worry that interfere with daily life. Panic attacks are sudden episodes of intense fear.

## Symptoms
Constant worry, restlessness, poor sleep, a racing heart, sweating, trembling, shortness of breath and chest tightness.

## Treatment approaches
Psychological therapy, relaxation and breathing techniques, and sometimes medicines prescribed by a health worker.

## When to seek medical attention
See a health worker if anxiety affects daily life. Chest pain or breathlessness should be checked by a health worker, because they can also be signs of physical illness.

## Danger signs
Vaginal bleeding; severe headache or blurred vision; swelling of the face and hands; fits; fever; severe abdominal pain; fluid leaking from the vagina before labour; the baby moving less than usual; difficulty breathing.

## When to seek medical attention
Any danger sign in pregnancy needs urgent care at a health facility.

## Antenatal care
Regular antenatal visits allow health workers to check blood pressure, test for anaemia, HIV and syphilis, give tetanus vaccine and supplements, and plan a safe birth with a skilled birth attendant.

## Definition
Pre-eclampsia is high blood pressure with signs of organ damage (often protein in the urine) that usually starts after 20 weeks of pregnancy. Eclampsia is when fits occur.

## Symptoms
Often none at first. Later: severe headache, vision problems, pain under the ribs, sudden swelling of the face and hands.

## How it is investigated
Blood pressure checks and urine tests at antenatal visits, and blood tests.

## Treatment approaches
Close monitoring and hospital care; the definitive treatment is delivery of the baby, timed by the medical team.

## When to seek medical attention
Urgent care for any symptom; fits are an emergency.

## General danger signs
WHO lists these general danger signs in children: not able to drink or breastfeed, vomiting everything, convulsions (fits), and being lethargic or unconscious. A child with any of these needs urgent care.

## Other urgent signs
Fast or difficult breathing, chest in-drawing, fever in a young baby, signs of severe dehydration, a stiff neck, and severe pallor or swelling.

## When to seek medical attention
Take the child to a health facility immediately for any danger sign.

## Definition
Measles is a highly contagious viral disease that mostly affects children and can be serious.

## Symptoms
High fever, cough, runny nose, red watery eyes, then a rash that starts on the face and spreads.

## Complications
Pneumonia, diarrhoea, eye damage, brain inflammation and death, especially in malnourished children.

## Treatment approaches
Supportive care, nutrition and vitamin A as recommended by health workers.

## Prevention
Two doses of measles-containing vaccine.

## When to seek medical attention
See a health worker for fever with rash. Urgent for breathing difficulty, dehydration or drowsiness.

## Definition
Malnutrition includes undernutrition (stunting, wasting, underweight), lack of vitamins and minerals, and overweight.

## Signs
Poor growth, very thin arms (measured with a MUAC tape), swollen feet (oedema), frequent infections and low energy.

## Treatment approaches
Assessment and nutrition programmes, including therapeutic foods for severe acute malnutrition, at health facilities.

## Prevention
Exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months, then varied complementary foods with continued breastfeeding, growth monitoring, vaccination and clean water.

## When to seek medical attention
Urgent for swollen feet, severe thinness, or a child who is not eating.

## Definition
Cancer is a group of diseases in which abnormal cells grow out of control and can spread to other parts of the body.

## Risk factors
Tobacco, harmful alcohol use, unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, air pollution, and some infections such as HPV and hepatitis B.

## Possible early signs
A lump, unexplained weight loss, a sore that does not heal, unusual bleeding, a long-lasting cough or hoarseness, and changes in bowel or bladder habits. These signs usually have other causes but should be checked.

## How it is investigated
Examination, imaging and a biopsy (a tissue sample) to confirm the diagnosis.

## Treatment approaches
Surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy and other medicines, chosen by specialist teams, plus palliative care to relieve symptoms.

## Prevention and screening
Avoid tobacco, HPV vaccination, hepatitis B vaccination, and screening such as cervical cancer screening as recommended by health services.

## When to seek medical attention
See a health worker promptly for any possible early sign that lasts more than a few weeks.
