## Definition
Malaria is a life-threatening disease caused by Plasmodium parasites. It spreads to people through the bites of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes. It is preventable and curable.

## Causes and risk factors
Caused by Plasmodium parasites, mainly P. falciparum in Africa. Higher risk: children under 5, pregnant women, people with HIV, and travellers without immunity.

## Symptoms
Fever, chills, headache, tiredness, muscle aches, nausea, vomiting or diarrhoea, usually 10 to 15 days after the mosquito bite.

## Warning signs and complications
Severe malaria can cause confusion, fits, coma, difficulty breathing, severe anaemia, dark urine, yellow eyes or skin, and kidney failure. It can kill quickly, especially in young children.

## How it is investigated
A blood test confirms malaria: a rapid diagnostic test (RDT) or microscopy. In Rwanda, community health workers and health centres can test for malaria.

## Treatment approaches
Malaria is treated with antimalarial medicines chosen by a health worker after a positive test, following national guidelines. Severe malaria needs hospital treatment, usually with injectable medicines.

## Prevention
Sleep under an insecticide-treated mosquito net every night, allow indoor spraying, remove standing water, and use preventive medicines or the malaria vaccine for groups where health services recommend them.

## When to seek medical attention
Get tested the same day for any fever where malaria is possible. Seek emergency care for any warning sign.

## Definition
TB is an infection caused by bacteria (Mycobacterium tuberculosis) that most often affects the lungs. It spreads through the air when people with lung TB cough. It is preventable and curable.

## Risk factors
HIV, undernutrition, diabetes, smoking, harmful alcohol use, and living with someone who has TB.

## Symptoms
A cough lasting more than 2 weeks, sometimes with blood, fever, night sweats, weight loss, chest pain and tiredness.

## How it is investigated
Sputum tests, including rapid molecular tests, and chest X-ray.

## Treatment approaches
A course of several antibiotics taken for months under the supervision of health services. Completing the full course is essential to cure TB and prevent drug resistance. TB services are provided by national programmes.

## Prevention
BCG vaccination for babies, early diagnosis and treatment, good ventilation, and preventive treatment for people at high risk as advised by health workers.

## When to seek medical attention
See a health worker for a cough lasting more than 2 weeks. Seek emergency care for coughing up large amounts of blood or severe breathlessness.

## Definition
HIV (human immunodeficiency virus) attacks the immune system. Without treatment it can progress to AIDS, the most advanced stage, when serious infections and some cancers develop.

## How it spreads
Through blood, semen, vaginal fluids and breast milk: unprotected sex, sharing needles, and from mother to child during pregnancy, birth or breastfeeding. It does not spread through hugging, sharing food or mosquito bites.

## Symptoms
Many people have no symptoms for years. Early infection can cause a flu-like illness. Later signs include weight loss, fever, diarrhoea, cough and repeated infections such as TB.

## How it is investigated
HIV testing with rapid tests confirmed according to national guidelines. Testing is the only way to know your status.

## Treatment approaches
Antiretroviral therapy (ART) taken every day controls the virus. People on effective treatment can live long, healthy lives, and those with an undetectable viral load do not pass HIV to sexual partners.

## Prevention
Condoms, HIV testing, pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) for people at higher risk, post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) started as soon as possible within 72 hours after a possible exposure, voluntary medical male circumcision, and treatment to prevent mother-to-child transmission.

## When to seek medical attention
Get tested if you may have been exposed. Seek care urgently (within 72 hours) after a possible exposure to ask about PEP.

## Definition
Typhoid is a serious infection caused by Salmonella Typhi bacteria, spread through contaminated food and water.

## Symptoms
Prolonged fever, tiredness, headache, nausea, abdominal pain, and constipation or diarrhoea. Some people get a rash.

## Complications
Without treatment it can cause bleeding or a hole in the intestine and can be fatal.

## How it is investigated
Blood or stool culture at a health facility. Typhoid cannot be reliably diagnosed from symptoms alone.

## Treatment approaches
Antibiotics prescribed by a health worker. Drug resistance is increasing, so the choice of antibiotic depends on local guidance.

## Prevention
Safe drinking water, hand washing with soap, safe food, good sanitation, and typhoid vaccination where recommended.

## When to seek medical attention
Seek care for fever lasting more than 3 days. Seek emergency care for severe abdominal pain, vomiting blood or confusion.

## Definition
Cholera is an acute diarrhoeal infection caused by Vibrio cholerae bacteria in contaminated water or food. It can kill within hours if untreated.

## Symptoms
Sudden, large amounts of watery diarrhoea, vomiting and rapid dehydration.

## How it is investigated
During outbreaks it is identified by symptoms and confirmed with stool tests.

## Treatment approaches
Prompt oral rehydration solution (ORS) treats most people. Severe cases need intravenous fluids at a health facility, and some need antibiotics.

## Prevention
Safe water, hand washing with soap, safe sanitation, safe food, and oral cholera vaccine where recommended.

## When to seek medical attention
Start ORS immediately and go to a health facility for severe watery diarrhoea, especially with signs of dehydration.

## Definition
COVID-19 is an infectious disease caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus, spread mainly through the air between people in close contact.

## Symptoms
Fever, cough, sore throat, tiredness, loss of taste or smell, and body aches. Older people and those with long-term conditions are at higher risk of severe illness.

## How it is investigated
Rapid antigen tests or PCR tests.

## Treatment approaches
Most people recover at home with rest and fluids. People at high risk or with severe illness may need medicines and oxygen in hospital, as decided by health workers.

## Prevention
Vaccination as recommended, staying home when sick, good ventilation, hand hygiene, and masks in crowded indoor spaces when advised.

## When to seek medical attention
Get emergency help for difficulty breathing, chest pain, confusion, or low oxygen saturation.

## Definition
Hepatitis B is a liver infection caused by the hepatitis B virus. It can be short-term or become long-term (chronic) and lead to liver scarring (cirrhosis) and liver cancer.

## How it spreads
Through blood and body fluids: from mother to baby at birth, unprotected sex, and sharing needles or other sharp objects.

## Symptoms
Many people have no symptoms. Some have yellow skin or eyes (jaundice), dark urine, tiredness, nausea and abdominal pain.

## How it is investigated
Blood tests for hepatitis B infection and liver function.

## Treatment approaches
Chronic hepatitis B can be controlled with antiviral medicines prescribed by specialists; not everyone needs treatment.

## Prevention
Hepatitis B vaccine (including a birth dose), safe blood and injections, and condoms.

## When to seek medical attention
Seek care for jaundice, dark urine or abdominal swelling, and ask about testing if a family member has hepatitis B.

## Definition
Pneumonia is an infection that inflames the air sacs in one or both lungs, which may fill with fluid or pus. It can be caused by bacteria, viruses or fungi.

## Risk factors
Young children, older adults, malnutrition, HIV, smoking, indoor air pollution, and long-term heart or lung disease.

## Symptoms
Cough, fever, fast or difficult breathing, chest pain when breathing. In children: fast breathing and chest in-drawing.

## How it is investigated
Examination, counting the breathing rate, oxygen saturation, and sometimes chest X-ray or blood tests.

## Treatment approaches
Bacterial pneumonia is treated with antibiotics prescribed by a health worker. Severe pneumonia needs hospital care and oxygen.

## Prevention
Vaccines (pneumococcal, Hib, measles, whooping cough), exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months, good nutrition, and reducing indoor smoke.

## When to seek medical attention
Seek care for cough with fast breathing or fever. Emergency for chest in-drawing, blue lips, inability to drink, or drowsiness.

## Definition
Asthma is a long-term lung condition in which the airways become inflamed and narrow, making it hard to breathe.

## Triggers and risk factors
Colds, dust, smoke, pollen, cold air, exercise, and strong smells. It often runs in families and is linked to allergies.

## Symptoms
Wheezing, cough (often at night), chest tightness and shortness of breath that come and go.

## How it is investigated
History and examination, and lung function tests such as peak flow or spirometry.

## Treatment approaches
Inhalers prescribed by a health worker: "reliever" inhalers open the airways quickly, and "preventer" inhalers reduce inflammation over time. An asthma action plan explains what to do when symptoms get worse.

## Prevention
Avoid known triggers and smoke, take preventer medicine as prescribed, and keep follow-up visits.

## When to seek medical attention
An asthma attack is an emergency if the reliever does not help, the person cannot speak in sentences, lips turn blue, or they become drowsy.

## Definition
COPD is a long-term lung disease that causes airflow blockage and breathing problems. It includes chronic bronchitis and emphysema.

## Causes and risk factors
Tobacco smoking, indoor smoke from cooking with wood or charcoal, workplace dust and fumes, and repeated childhood lung infections.

## Symptoms
Breathlessness, especially on activity, long-term cough with phlegm, wheezing and frequent chest infections.

## How it is investigated
Spirometry (a breathing test) confirms the diagnosis.

## Treatment approaches
Stopping smoking, inhaled medicines, pulmonary rehabilitation (exercise and breathing training), vaccines, and oxygen for some people.

## Prevention
Avoid tobacco and indoor smoke; use clean cooking fuels and ventilation.

## When to seek medical attention
Seek urgent care for breathing that is much worse than usual, blue lips, confusion or chest pain.
