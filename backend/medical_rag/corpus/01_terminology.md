## How medical words are built
Many medical words are made of parts. A prefix comes at the start, a root names the body part, and a suffix comes at the end. For example, "gastr-itis" means inflammation (-itis) of the stomach (gastr-).

## Common prefixes
hyper- means too much or above normal (hypertension = high blood pressure). hypo- means too little or below normal (hypoglycaemia = low blood sugar). tachy- means fast (tachycardia = fast heart rate). brady- means slow (bradycardia = slow heart rate). dys- means difficult or abnormal (dyspnoea = difficulty breathing). a- or an- means without (anaemia = lack of healthy red blood cells).

## Common body-part roots
cardi- heart; pulmon- or pneum- lung; gastr- stomach; enter- intestine; hepat- liver; nephr- or ren- kidney; neur- nerve; derm- skin; haem- or hem- blood; oste- bone; arthr- joint; encephal- brain; cyst- bladder.

## Common suffixes
-itis inflammation (appendicitis). -ectomy surgical removal (appendectomy). -ology study of (cardiology). -algia pain (myalgia = muscle pain). -emia or -aemia condition of the blood (septicaemia). -osis abnormal condition (tuberculosis). -scopy looking inside with an instrument (endoscopy).

## Vital-sign abbreviations
BP blood pressure. HR heart rate (pulse). RR respiratory rate (breaths per minute). SpO2 oxygen saturation measured with a pulse oximeter. T or Temp body temperature. bpm beats per minute. mmHg millimetres of mercury, the unit for blood pressure.

## Test and treatment abbreviations
ECG or EKG electrocardiogram, a recording of the heart's electrical activity. CBC or FBC complete or full blood count. BMI body mass index. IV intravenous (into a vein). IM intramuscular (into a muscle). ORS oral rehydration solution. RDT rapid diagnostic test (for example for malaria).

## Places and prescription abbreviations
ICU intensive care unit. ER or ED emergency room or department. OPD outpatient department. On prescriptions, "bid" means twice a day, "tid" three times a day and "prn" as needed. Always follow the instructions your health worker or pharmacist gives you for your own medicine.

## Complete blood count (CBC)
Counts red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets, and measures haemoglobin. It helps check for anaemia, infection and some blood disorders. A result outside the reference range does not always mean disease; a health worker interprets it with your symptoms.

## Blood glucose and HbA1c
Blood glucose measures sugar in the blood at one moment (fasting or random). HbA1c shows the average blood sugar over about the past 2 to 3 months. Both are used to diagnose and monitor diabetes.

## Kidney and liver tests
Creatinine and urea in the blood show how well the kidneys filter waste. Liver function tests (such as ALT, AST and bilirubin) show whether the liver is inflamed or working normally.

## Urine tests and rapid tests
A urinalysis looks at urine for signs of infection, blood, protein or sugar. Rapid diagnostic tests (RDTs) give fast results for some infections, such as malaria and HIV, and are confirmed or followed up according to national guidelines.

## Reference ranges
Each laboratory reports a "reference range" for its own equipment. Results should always be explained by a health professional, who considers age, sex, pregnancy, medicines and symptoms.

## Heart and blood vessels (cardiovascular system)
The heart pumps blood through arteries to the whole body and receives it back through veins. Blood carries oxygen and nutrients to tissues and removes waste.

## Lungs (respiratory system)
The lungs take in oxygen from the air and remove carbon dioxide. Oxygen passes into the blood in tiny air sacs called alveoli.

## Brain and nerves (nervous system)
The brain, spinal cord and nerves control movement, feeling, thinking, breathing and heart rate. They send signals around the body.

## Digestive system
The mouth, oesophagus, stomach and intestines break down food and absorb nutrients. The liver makes bile, processes nutrients and removes toxins. The pancreas makes digestive enzymes and insulin.

## Kidneys and urinary system
The kidneys filter waste and extra water from the blood to make urine, and help control blood pressure and the body's salt balance. Urine is stored in the bladder.

## Endocrine system
Glands such as the pancreas, thyroid and adrenal glands release hormones that control energy use, growth, blood sugar and stress responses.

## Immune system and skin
White blood cells, lymph nodes and the spleen defend the body against infection. The skin is a barrier that protects the body and helps control temperature.
