## Definition
Hypertension means blood pressure that is too high, usually 140/90 mmHg or higher on repeated measurements. It is common and often called a "silent" condition because most people have no symptoms.

## Causes and risk factors
Often no single cause. Risk factors: eating too much salt, being overweight, physical inactivity, tobacco and alcohol use, older age, family history, diabetes and kidney disease.

## Symptoms
Usually none. Very high blood pressure may cause severe headache, chest pain, dizziness, vision changes or nosebleeds.

## Complications
Over time: heart attack, heart failure, stroke, kidney damage and eye damage.

## How it is investigated
Blood pressure measurement on several occasions, sometimes home or 24-hour monitoring, plus tests for kidney function, blood sugar and cholesterol.

## Treatment approaches
Lifestyle changes (less salt, healthy weight, regular exercise, no tobacco, less alcohol) and blood-pressure medicines prescribed and adjusted by a health worker. Medicines should not be stopped without medical advice.

## Prevention
Eat less salt, more fruit and vegetables, stay active, avoid tobacco, limit alcohol, and check your blood pressure regularly.

## When to seek medical attention
Get emergency help for very high readings with chest pain, severe headache, weakness, confusion or vision loss.

## Definition
Hypotension is blood pressure that is lower than normal, generally below 90/60 mmHg.

## Causes
It can be normal for some healthy people. Other causes: dehydration, blood loss, severe infection (sepsis), severe allergic reaction, heart problems, pregnancy, standing up quickly (postural hypotension) and some medicines.

## Symptoms
Dizziness, fainting, blurred vision, nausea, tiredness, and in shock: cold clammy skin, fast weak pulse, fast breathing and confusion.

## How it is investigated
Blood pressure measured lying and standing, and tests to find the cause.

## Treatment approaches
Depends on the cause, for example fluids for dehydration or reviewing medicines with a health worker.

## When to seek medical attention
Emergency for signs of shock, fainting with injury, or low blood pressure after bleeding or with fever.

## Definition
Coronary heart disease happens when fatty deposits narrow the arteries that supply the heart. A heart attack (myocardial infarction) occurs when blood flow to part of the heart is blocked.

## Risk factors
High blood pressure, diabetes, high cholesterol, tobacco, unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, harmful alcohol use, obesity, older age and family history.

## Symptoms
Chest pain or pressure, pain in the arms, jaw, neck or back, shortness of breath, sweating, nausea and light-headedness. Women, older people and people with diabetes may have milder or unusual symptoms.

## How it is investigated
ECG, blood tests (troponin) and heart imaging in hospital.

## Treatment approaches
Emergency hospital treatment to restore blood flow, followed by long-term medicines, lifestyle changes and cardiac rehabilitation.

## Prevention
Control blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol; avoid tobacco; eat healthily; stay active.

## When to seek medical attention
A suspected heart attack is an emergency: request an ambulance immediately.

## Definition
Heart failure means the heart cannot pump blood as well as the body needs. It does not mean the heart has stopped.

## Causes
Coronary heart disease, high blood pressure, heart-valve disease, rheumatic heart disease, cardiomyopathy and some infections.

## Symptoms
Breathlessness (especially when lying flat or on activity), swollen ankles and legs, tiredness and rapid weight gain from fluid.

## How it is investigated
Examination, chest X-ray, ECG, echocardiogram (heart ultrasound) and blood tests.

## Treatment approaches
Long-term medicines, salt and fluid advice, and treating the cause, managed by a health worker.

## When to seek medical attention
Emergency for severe breathlessness, chest pain or fainting. Seek prompt care for quickly increasing swelling or weight.

## Definition
A stroke happens when blood supply to part of the brain is blocked (ischaemic stroke) or a blood vessel in the brain bursts (haemorrhagic stroke).

## Risk factors
High blood pressure (the most important), diabetes, smoking, atrial fibrillation, high cholesterol, older age, and previous stroke.

## Signs (FAST)
Face drooping on one side, Arm or leg weakness or numbness, Speech slurred or confused, Time to request emergency help. Also sudden vision loss, severe headache or loss of balance.

## How it is investigated
Brain scan (CT or MRI) in hospital, as fast as possible.

## Treatment approaches
Some treatments only work within hours of the first symptom, so speed matters. Rehabilitation (physiotherapy, speech therapy) helps recovery.

## When to seek medical attention
Every suspected stroke is an emergency. Note the time symptoms started and do not give food or drink.

## Definition
Epilepsy is a long-term brain condition causing repeated seizures (fits): brief episodes of abnormal electrical activity in the brain.

## Causes
Brain injury, infections such as meningitis or cerebral malaria, stroke, birth injuries, genetic causes; often no cause is found.

## Symptoms
Shaking, stiffness, loss of awareness, staring spells, or unusual sensations.

## How it is investigated
A detailed description of the episodes, EEG and sometimes brain imaging.

## Treatment approaches
Anti-seizure medicines prescribed by a health worker control seizures in most people.

## First aid during a seizure
Keep the person safe from injury, cushion the head, do not put anything in the mouth, time the seizure, and turn them on their side afterwards.

## When to seek medical attention
Emergency for a seizure lasting more than 5 minutes, repeated seizures, a first-ever seizure, injury, or difficulty breathing afterwards.

## Definition
Meningitis is inflammation of the membranes covering the brain and spinal cord, usually caused by infection. Bacterial meningitis can kill within hours.

## Symptoms
Fever, severe headache, stiff neck, sensitivity to light, confusion, vomiting, seizures, and sometimes a rash. Babies may be floppy, irritable or have a bulging soft spot on the head.

## How it is investigated
Lumbar puncture and blood tests in hospital.

## Treatment approaches
Urgent hospital treatment with antibiotics for bacterial meningitis.

## Prevention
Vaccines (meningococcal, pneumococcal, Hib).

## When to seek medical attention
Suspected meningitis is an emergency: seek help immediately.

## Definition
Migraine is a common headache disorder with repeated attacks of moderate to severe, often throbbing, headache.

## Symptoms
Headache often on one side, nausea, sensitivity to light and sound. Some people have an "aura" (flashing lights or zigzag lines) before the headache.

## Triggers
Stress, lack of sleep, skipped meals, hormonal changes and some foods.

## Treatment approaches
Rest in a dark quiet room, pain-relief medicines and, for frequent attacks, preventive treatment advised by a health worker. Overusing pain medicines can cause more headaches.

## When to seek medical attention
Emergency for a sudden "worst ever" headache, or headache with weakness, confusion or loss of vision.

## Definition
Diabetes is a long-term condition in which blood sugar (glucose) is too high because the body does not make enough insulin (type 1) or cannot use insulin properly (type 2). Gestational diabetes occurs in pregnancy.

## Risk factors
Type 2: overweight, physical inactivity, unhealthy diet, older age, family history and previous gestational diabetes.

## Symptoms
Feeling very thirsty, passing urine often, tiredness, blurred vision, weight loss, slow-healing wounds and frequent infections. Type 2 can have no symptoms for years.

## Complications
Damage to the heart, blood vessels, eyes, kidneys and nerves; foot ulcers; very high blood sugar emergencies (diabetic ketoacidosis).

## How it is investigated
Fasting or random blood glucose and HbA1c tests.

## Treatment approaches
Healthy eating, physical activity, weight management, medicines or insulin prescribed by a health worker, and regular checks of blood sugar, eyes, feet and kidneys.

## Prevention
For type 2: healthy weight, regular activity, healthy diet and no tobacco.

## When to seek medical attention
Emergency for confusion, drowsiness, deep fast breathing, vomiting with high sugar, or signs of low blood sugar that do not improve.

## Definition
Hypoglycaemia means blood sugar is too low, most often in people treated with insulin or some diabetes tablets.

## Symptoms
Shaking, sweating, hunger, fast heartbeat, irritability, confusion, and in severe cases seizures or unconsciousness.

## First aid
If the person is awake and able to swallow, give a sugary drink or food, then a meal or snack when they feel better.

## When to seek medical attention
Emergency if the person is unconscious, cannot swallow safely, or has a seizure. Do not give food or drink to someone who is unconscious.

## Definition
The thyroid gland in the neck makes hormones that control how the body uses energy. Hyperthyroidism means too much hormone; hypothyroidism means too little.

## Symptoms
Overactive: weight loss, fast heartbeat, sweating, anxiety, tremor. Underactive: tiredness, weight gain, feeling cold, constipation, dry skin. A swelling in the neck (goitre) can occur with either.

## How it is investigated
Blood tests for thyroid hormones (TSH, T4).

## Treatment approaches
Medicines prescribed and monitored by a health worker; some people need other treatments.

## When to seek medical attention
See a health worker for these symptoms or a neck swelling.
