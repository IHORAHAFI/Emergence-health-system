## Main categories
Pain relievers (analgesics) reduce pain and fever. Antibiotics treat bacterial infections; they do not work against viruses such as colds and flu. Antimalarials treat or prevent malaria. Antiretrovirals control HIV. Antihypertensives lower blood pressure. Antidiabetic medicines and insulin lower blood sugar. Bronchodilators open the airways in asthma and COPD. Antihistamines reduce allergy symptoms. Vaccines train the immune system to prevent infections.

## Important safety rule
This assistant gives general information only. It cannot recommend a medicine or a dose for you. Your doctor, nurse or pharmacist decides which medicine and dose is right for your age, weight, pregnancy status, other illnesses and other medicines.

## Side effects
All medicines can cause side effects. Common ones are nausea, stomach upset, drowsiness or headache. Serious reactions include swelling of the face or throat, difficulty breathing, severe rash or blistering, yellow eyes and dark urine.

## Interactions
Some medicines, herbal remedies and alcohol affect each other and can make a medicine stronger, weaker or harmful. Always tell your health worker and pharmacist about everything you take, including traditional and herbal products.

## Safe use
Take medicines exactly as prescribed, finish courses of antibiotics or TB treatment as instructed, do not share medicines, check expiry dates, and keep medicines away from children.

## Changing or stopping medicines
Do not stop or change a prescribed medicine on your own. Some medicines are dangerous to stop suddenly. Talk to your health worker first.

## When to seek medical attention
Emergency for signs of a severe allergic reaction or a suspected overdose.

## What antibiotics do
Antibiotics kill bacteria or stop them growing. They do not treat viral infections such as colds, most sore throats and flu.

## Antibiotic resistance
When antibiotics are used unnecessarily or incorrectly, bacteria can become resistant, so the medicines no longer work. This makes infections harder to treat.

## How to help
Only take antibiotics prescribed by a qualified health worker, follow the instructions, do not save them for later and do not share them.

## Check for danger and call for help
Make sure the area is safe, check whether the person responds and breathes normally, and request emergency help early.

## Main steps
Unresponsive and not breathing: start CPR. Unresponsive but breathing: recovery position. Heavy bleeding: press firmly on the wound. Burns: cool with running water for 20 minutes. Choking: back blows and abdominal thrusts.

## Training
First aid courses teach these skills with practice. Knowledge from a page is not a substitute for training.

## What rehabilitation is
Rehabilitation helps people recover or keep their ability to do daily activities after an illness, injury or surgery, or when living with a long-term condition.

## Examples
Physiotherapy (movement and strength), occupational therapy (daily tasks), speech and swallowing therapy, cardiac and pulmonary rehabilitation, and assistive devices.

## How to access it
Ask your health worker for referral to rehabilitation services.

## Non-surgical treatments
Many conditions are treated without surgery: medicines, physiotherapy, lifestyle changes, dietary changes, and procedures through small tubes (such as endoscopy).

## Surgical treatments
Surgery is an operation to repair, remove or replace tissue, done under anaesthesia. Examples include removing an appendix or setting a broken bone.

## Making decisions
The surgical team explains the benefits, risks and alternatives before you agree (informed consent). Ask questions until you understand.

## Vaccines
Vaccines protect against diseases such as measles, polio, tetanus, whooping cough, hepatitis B, pneumonia caused by pneumococcus, HPV and others. Follow the national immunization schedule for children and recommended vaccines for adults and pregnant women.

## Screening
Regular checks find problems early: blood pressure, blood sugar, HIV testing, cervical cancer screening, and growth monitoring in children.

## Healthy living
Eat a varied diet with fruit and vegetables and less salt, sugar and fat; be physically active; avoid tobacco; limit alcohol; sleep well; wash hands; drink safe water; and sleep under a mosquito net in malaria areas.

## Get emergency help immediately for
Not breathing or severe difficulty breathing; chest pain or pressure; signs of stroke (face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech); unconsciousness or sudden confusion; seizures; severe bleeding; severe burns; a serious injury; a severe allergic reaction; poisoning or overdose; thoughts of suicide; danger signs in pregnancy; and danger signs in a sick child.

## While waiting
Stay with the person, follow instructions from the emergency operator, and do not give food or drink to someone who is very drowsy or unconscious.
